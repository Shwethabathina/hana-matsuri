1. Plan vs. Reality

Our Assignment 2 MVP was a fully playable Goldfish Scooping (Kingyo-sukui) activity inside the festival world, with independently moving fish, a responsive paper scoop, fish–scoop collision, real-time water feedback without a full fluid solver, and a result that returns to the festival HUD. The success test was one continuous session: walk to stall → press E → control scoop → water reacts → catch fish → reward and objective complete. This sequence works in the live build.

Made it in

  • Animated fish: a rigged goldfish glTF model (8 animation clips) is cloned per fish with its own AnimationMixer; slow and fast swim cycles are blended by speed, and the jump clip plays when a fish is scooped into the bowl.
  • Fish behaviour: boids (separation, alignment, cohesion), tub-wall avoidance, and fleeing from the submerged scoop. Fish drift between surface and bottom and dive when frightened, so they stay inside the tank and move independently.
  • Water: a height-field simulation (discrete 2D wave equation on a 96×41 grid, fixed 60 Hz step, damping, reflecting borders). Dipping, dragging and lifting the scoop, caught fish and shallow-swimming fish create ripples that spread and reflect off the tub walls; vertex normals and crest colouring are rebuilt each step.
  • Collision: a fish is caught only if its body circle overlaps the paper disc and it is above the paper's depth when the scoop is lifted. Paper strength drops with water drag (scoop speed) and fish weight, and the paper visibly tears. A debug view (V) draws the catch radius and every fish's hit circle (green = catchable, red = too deep).
  • Game loop: results return to the festival: yen reward, prize, objective ticked, and a firework pattern unlocked for the finale.
  • Performance tools: live FPS, fish count, grid size and simulation cost in the HUD; P adds 20 fish, O raises the water resolution.

Cut or changed

  • We planned "ripple or height-field water"; we implemented the height field and dropped true refraction of the fish under the surface. The water is a translucent, reflective surface instead.
  • Fish collision uses 2D circles plus a depth check rather than mesh-accurate tests. It is cheaper and easier to read on screen.
  • The goldfish model's glass-like transmission material was disabled: it forced an extra full-scene render pass every frame.
  • Large assets were compressed for the web: the festival street from 97 MB to 23 MB and the goldfish from 12 MB to 3.5 MB.

2. AI Utility

  • Claude Code was the most useful tool. It produced working first versions of large systems quickly: the boids steering, the height-field water, the behaviour-tree NPCs, A* navigation, the fireworks particles, and the asset compression pipeline (gltf-transform). It also ran the game in a headless browser to take screenshots and catch runtime errors.
  • ChatGPT helped with debugging questions, Three.js API details and shader/particle ideas.
  • AI image generation was used only for concept mockups (visual reference), not for in-game assets.
  • Time saved vs. debugging: overall AI saved a lot of time on boilerplate and first drafts. The cost was in integration and tuning. Examples: the first night lighting was badly over-exposed and needed manual tuning; the default model compression merged meshes and broke our collision and lantern-glow lookups, so we had to change its settings; the goldfish model's material settings had to be found and disabled for performance; and game-feel values (scoop strength, flee distance, ripple size) still needed playtesting. AI output always needed checking in the running game.
  • No runtime AI API is used, as stated in our tech spec, so the build is predictable and works offline.

3. Roadmap to Final

  • Other main activities: polish the Shooting Gallery (cork ballistics, toppling prizes) and the Taiko rhythm game, and finish Kimodameshi (scares, courage seals, fog), which are currently playable prototypes.
  • Rendering: enable the island environment with reflective water, cliffs and the raised shrine terrace (drafted in island.js); add caustics or refraction to the goldfish tub; tune night lighting.
  • NPCs: extend the behaviour trees (queueing at stalls, reacting to the player's games) and the stall-owner states.
  • Short interactions: ring toss, yo-yo balloon fishing, mask memory, Daruma balancing, lantern-lighting puzzle (scope permitting).
  • Finale: make firework patterns depend on more of the player's progress; performance pass (instanced fish, GPU water) for lower-end laptops.
